LoveRaw collections: SEO strategy and plan

LoveRaw sells 25 products and has 28 collections. The plan is to keep fewer, stronger collections in search. The main nav carries product types and gifts. Thin, duplicate, empty and admin collections are kept out of the index. Checked live on 23 November 2026.

Issues on every collection (theme template)

  • Schema: the ItemList uses the invalid property "@numberOfItems" and lists full Product objects instead of ListItem entries with a position. In the breadcrumb, “home” is lowercase and the last item has no URL.
  • Headings: every product card, the cart drawer and the footer labels are H2s. Product cards should be H3, and the cart and footer headings should be plain text.
  • Social: og:title shows only the collection name, and 8 collections use Shopify’s placeholder image.
  • Related content: every collection shows the same 3 blog posts, including the “rescued from administration” press release.
  • Broken links: on the homepage, /collections/cre-m-wafer-bar redirects to a 404.

2. What goes in the main nav, and why

Links in the main nav appear on every page, so they carry the most internal link weight. They should go to the collections that are bigger, sell more and match the main searches. Today 2 of the 7 nav links point to duplicate collections that shouldn’t rank.

Flavours: a filter for shoppers, plus 2 landing pages for search

  • Add a Flavour filter to every collection, next to the current Availability, Brand and Price filters. Use Shopify Search & Discovery with a product metafield custom.flavour (a list, because some products have two flavours, like Salted Peanut Caramel). Values: Pistachio, Peanut, Hazelnut, Almond, Caramel, White Chocolate, Milk Chocolate, Double Chocolate, Vanilla, Berry, Spiced Orange.
  • The filter is for shoppers, not for search. Filter URLs (?filter.p.m.custom.flavour=…) canonicalise to the collection, so they don’t rank. This works for flavours with little search demand, like Hazelnut, Almond and Caramel, which is why those three collections can be noindexed.
  • Keep Pistachio and Peanut as indexed collections. They have real search demand (“vegan pistachio chocolate”, “vegan peanut butter chocolate”), and each has 3–4 products. A filter can’t rank for those searches; a collection page can. Milk and White Chocolate stay indexed for the same reason.
  • Keep filter URLs out of the index. Leave the self-canonical in place. Shopify’s default robots.txt already blocks URLs that combine several filters.

Indexed but left out of the nav

  • Milk Chocolate, White Chocolate, Pistachio, Peanut: these have their own search demand but only 3–7 products, and they overlap the product-type pages. Adding them would double the size of the nav. Instead, link to them from a “Shop by type & flavour” row at the top of Shop all, from related product-type pages, and from product pages (for example, Pistachio Bliss links to Spreads and Pistachio).
  • Halloween: it goes in the Gifts slot in October only. The rest of the year, link to it from Vegan Gifts.
  • Footer: add a Shop column with Shop all, Wafer Bars, Butter Cups, Spreads and Vegan Gifts. The footer has no shop links today.

How to apply noindex

  • In Shopify, add the collection metafield seo.hidden (integer, value 1). This adds noindex and also drops the page from sitemap.xml. Do the same for the 3 collections that are already noindex, because the noindex they have today leaves them in the sitemap.
  • For all, change the canonical back to the page’s own URL. A noindex together with a canonical to another page sends mixed signals.
  • Don’t block these URLs in robots.txt. Google has to crawl the page to see the noindex.
  • Before you noindex Hazelnut, Almond and Caramel, check Search Console. If a page has had steady clicks over the last 12 months, keep it indexed and improve it instead.

Plan

In priority order

Steps 1–3 should go live before mid-December.

  1. Fill Vegan Gifts and Christmas with published products. Add the 3 missing products to Pistachio.
  2. Update the main nav: Shop all → vegan-chocolate, Cookies → vegan-cookies, and a new Gifts group. Change the announcement bar link too.
  3. Apply seo.hidden to the 11 collections in section 3 and the 3 that are already noindex. Fix the canonical on all.
  4. Fix the broken homepage link and add links in content: a type and flavour row on Shop all, related collections on type pages, collection links on product pages, and a Shop column in the footer.
  5. Add the Flavour filter: create the custom.flavour metafield, tag all 25 products with their flavours, and turn the filter on in Search & Discovery.
  6. Update the metadata for Best Sellers, Mixed Boxes, Vegan Cookies, Pistachio, Milk Chocolate and Christmas (section 1).
  7. Fix the theme template: ItemList and breadcrumb schema, FAQ JSON, heading levels and og tags.
  8. Submit the sitemap again in Search Console. Check that the noindexed pages drop out of the index within 2–4 weeks.
